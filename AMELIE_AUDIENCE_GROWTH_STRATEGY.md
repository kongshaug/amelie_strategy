## 1. Executive Summary

### The decision

Amelie should position herself as **the contemporary tattoo artist who turns movement, emotion, and human stories into one-off art that lives on the body**. The growth product is not a booking feed. It is a repeatable creator world built from recognizable art, visible process, Amelie's face and voice, and audience participation.

Instagram remains the primary conversion home. TikTok becomes the second-ranked discovery laboratory, starting from about 20 followers. Copenhagen supplies collaborators, cultural relevance, and physical activations, but the target audience remains pan-European and English-first. Bookings are a secondary consequence, not the editorial objective.

### What the evidence says

- The starting count is 17,322. Instagram reported +1,344 net followers and +8.4% over the latest 90 days, so the account is growing, but more slowly than the approximate six-month movement from 12,000 to 17,000 suggests [A07].
- The account produced 1,052,204 views, 25,692 profile visits, and about 413,516 non-follower views in 90 days. The derived account funnel was 1.28 net follows per 1,000 total views and 5.23% net follows per profile visit [A07].
- Ordinary sampled Reels reached 51.6%-75.6% non-followers. Discovery exists; weak conversion into profile visits and follows is the main controllable bottleneck [A07].
- The two face-led, unclaimed-design Reels produced 332 and 181 attributed follows, but they were likely boosted at about EUR 10/day for roughly two weeks. Spend allocation, targeting, paid reach, and the organic/paid split are unknown. They are **paid-assisted hypotheses, not proven organic winners** [A07, A08].
- Carousels and posts cannot be treated as support content. They generated 2.93 times the aggregate Reel views and 3.54 times the aggregate Reel interactions in the screenshots, although publishing volume by format is unknown. One placement/design carousel generated 47 attributed follows [A07].
- The audience is already international: Germany 26.4%, Denmark 15.2%, France 8.4%, Sweden 7.0%, and the United States 5.0%. Women are 78.9%; ages 18-34 are 85.6% [A07].

### The operating strategy

1. In Weeks 1-4, publish two Instagram Reels and one carousel per week; in Weeks 5-12, add at most a third Reel only after repeat winners emerge and the week still fits the 6-10 hour capacity. Use Stories on 4-5 days.
2. Publish three TikToks per week from shared footage until the 30-post gate, giving one a more native treatment where possible without adding a separate shoot.
3. Publish only the 1-2 strongest clean edits as YouTube Shorts when each adaptation takes 10 minutes or less; Shorts are not a fixed production burden.
4. Show Amelie speaking or reacting in roughly two-thirds of Reels, with at least one face- or voice-led Reel in a two-Reel week.
5. Build named, recurring series around movement-to-design, unclaimed art, placement decisions, and the honest creative process.
6. Aim for one properly structured Instagram Collab every 2-4 weeks and complete 3-5 tailored outreach actions each week.
7. Spend nothing on paid distribution before organic validation. Month 1 may use only EUR 30-150 in small tests if a creative qualifies; repeated winners may later receive up to 60% of deployed budget, with no obligation to spend and a EUR 150 per-creative review cap.
8. Judge content by attributed follows per 1,000 total views, profile visits per 1,000 views, non-follower share, and shares, not likes or bookings.

### Resource allocation

The base operating plan uses 7.5 hours per week, inside the confirmed 6-10 hour capacity:

| Work | Hours | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Batch filming and photography | 2.5 | 33% |
| Editing and cross-platform adaptation | 2.25 | 30% |
| Publishing and community replies | 1.0 | 13% |
| Collaboration and PR outreach | 1.0 | 13% |
| Analytics and next-week decisions | 0.75 | 10% |

If only six hours are available, hold the Weeks 1-4 baseline of two Reels and one carousel, keep TikTok to shared-footage adaptations, use four purposeful Story days, skip Shorts and the optional third Reel, and preserve measurement.

## 2. Current Social Media Diagnosis

### Funnel diagnosis

| Stage | First-party evidence | Diagnosis | Required change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attention | 1,052,204 views in 90 days | Meaningful activity already exists | Create stronger opening tension and recognizable series |
| Discovery | 39.3% of account views from non-followers; sampled Reels reached 51.6%-75.6% non-followers | Instagram is distributing the work beyond followers | Increase repeatable non-follower reach, not random volume |
| Profile interest | 25,692 profile visits | People investigate, but source attribution is incomplete | Make every post and the profile explain why to follow |
| Follow conversion | 1,344 net follows; derived 1.28 per 1,000 total views and 5.23% per profile visit | Conversion is the principal bottleneck | Give viewers a serial promise, not only a finished tattoo |
| Repeat audience | 7.21 views per viewer, derived | Repeat exposure may exist, but repeat-viewer data is absent | Use named series and recurring visual grammar |
| Booking | 285 bio-link taps; secondary objective | Website-led posts do not grow followers | Move booking prompts to bio, Highlights, and Stories |

### Strongest current signals

- **Face plus design tension:** the two likely paid-assisted Reels combined face-led delivery with a clear unresolved question: why desirable original designs remained unclaimed. This is the best creative hypothesis, not proof of organic performance [A07, A08].
- **Clear artistic identity:** "I do abstract linework and dancing figures" was the strongest fully documented ordinary Reel, with 10 follows from 6,537 views, 97 profile visits, and 67.5% non-followers [A07].
- **Design and placement carousels:** available designs, body placement, and strong finished placements repeatedly appear among top posts [A07].
- **Cultural relevance:** "Trampstamps are so back" had the longest shown average watch time at 10 seconds, but duration is unknown, so completion cannot be inferred [A07].

### Weakest current signals

- Generic tattoo vlogs, guest-spot notices, thank-you posts, website announcements, and contextless finished work generate little follower conversion in the visible sample [A07].
- Existing collaboration Reels concentrated Danish viewers more than they transferred followers: the Kult Tattoo Kollektiv Reel generated three follows and the Studio Spiced Reel two [A07]. The lesson is to redesign the collaboration concept, hook, joint publishing, and follow promise, not to abandon collaboration.
- A website announcement produced zero follows while generating 31 link clicks. It did its traffic job, but traffic content should not consume much growth-feed capacity [A07].

### Known, unknown, and assumed

**Known**

- Starting count: 17,322.
- Capacity: 6-10 hours per week.
- Test budget: EUR 250-750 per month.
- TikTok: recently started, about 20 followers, no useful baseline.
- Amelie can speak English and German and is comfortable on camera.
- Art and creative process are the only confirmed authentic adjacent topics.

**Unknown**

- Paid versus organic delivery and follows for the 332/181-follow Reels.
- Reel durations, completion rates, repeat viewers, Story-level results, and gross follows versus unfollows.
- Exact posting volume by format, sources of profile visits, and the content behind the earlier six-month jump.
- Client-filming permissions and the safe supply of client stories.
- Whether any topic beyond tattooing, art, and creative process is authentic enough to sustain.

**Operating assumptions**

- English is the primary language for pan-European discovery. German is a controlled test, not a second full content operation.
- Client privacy and written consent override content value.
- The two face-led outliers remain classified as paid-assisted until promotion screens show otherwise.
- Copenhagen is a context and distribution network, not the total addressable audience.

The highest-value missing inputs remain the two promotion-result screens and duration/retention screens for the leading Reels. Strategy decisions should be revised when those arrive.

### What true outliers add

Three verified tattoo-content outliers show that tattoo quality alone does not create mass reach:

- A freckle-sized heart used extreme scale and surprise; it had nearly 24 million TikTok views when reported [V01].
- An IKEA-instruction tattoo used a universally understood cultural frame and an outcome reveal; it had nearly 9 million views and more than 1 million likes when reported [V02].
- The "Crazy Aunt Rose" identity line turned a tattoo into a label people recognized and shared; it had 18.8 million views in one day when reported [V03].

The recurring competitive pattern is **distinctive art + a simple story mechanism + a creator or identity people can remember**. Amelie has the first component; the strategy supplies the second and third.

## 6. Amelie's Potential Content Moat

### The moat stack

1. **Visual asset:** abstract linework, dancing figures, movement, softness, and body-responsive composition [A01].
2. **Process asset:** collaboration between drawing and body rather than applying a fixed image anywhere [A01].
3. **Creator asset:** Amelie is willing to show her face and speak, while much portfolio content remains anonymous [A07].
4. **Narrative asset:** a German artist building a creative life in Copenhagen, useful when attached to art rather than generic relocation lifestyle.
5. **Scarcity asset:** one-off flash and unclaimed original designs create honest tension [A01, A07].
6. **World-building asset:** Wabbelis and other recurring figures can develop lore rather than remain isolated motifs [A03].

### What Amelie can become known for

> Amelie turns movement, emotion, and small human stories into one-off contemporary art that comes alive on the body.

This is narrower and more ownable than "Copenhagen tattoo artist," but broad enough to attract people who follow art and process without intending to book.

### The recurring universe

- **From Movement to Mark:** movement reference -> first lines -> design -> body.
- **Nobody Claimed This:** Amelie presents one original, why she cares about it, and the placement she imagines; a later episode reveals its owner.
- **One Body, Three Placements:** the same idea changes with three body placements; the audience chooses.
- **Wabbeli Field Notes:** a recurring figure gets a name, behavior, origin, and real-world form.
- **The Honest Studio:** one real creative decision, failed draft, doubt, or constraint and the resulting choice.

The moat is not merely a visual style. It is a repeatable way of seeing that competitors cannot reproduce without copying Amelie's art, voice, and history.

### Guardrail

Do not manufacture a broader lifestyle persona. Fashion, beauty, relationships, and city life are unconfirmed. They can enter through a relevant collaborator or a real art story, not as filler.

## 8. Instagram Strategy

### Role

Instagram is the primary home for follower conversion because it already has 17,322 followers, documented discovery, visual proof, and profile depth. Reels create discovery; carousels establish authorship, saves, and profile confidence; Stories deepen repeat behavior.

### Exact cadence

| Phase | Instagram feed | Stories | Capacity rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1-4 | 2 Reels and 1 carousel per week | 4 days per week | This is the base cadence and organic-testing phase. |
| Weeks 5-12 | Up to 3 Reels and 1 carousel per week | 4-5 days per week | Add the third Reel only when repeat winners exist and total work remains within 6-10 hours. |

A practical base rhythm is Tuesday and Thursday Reels, Saturday carousel, and purposeful Stories around process, polls, replies, and reposts. An optional third Reel can run on Sunday in Weeks 5-12. A ready Collab replaces a scheduled Reel rather than adding another production, with one Collab targeted every 2-4 weeks. Publishing times should come from Instagram's audience-active data and testing; no unsupported universal "best time" is assumed.

### Reels system

- Default to 9-20 seconds for one-idea clips; use 20-40 seconds only when a story has real progression.
- Put the face, artwork, or unresolved result in the first second. State the specific tension in on-screen text.
- Deliver visual progress by second three; do not open with a logo, street shot, coffee, or room setup.
- Use spoken English and burned-in English subtitles. Keep source files clean for TikTok and Shorts.
- Show Amelie in roughly two-thirds of Reels: at least one of two in the base phase and normally two of three in a scaled week. Face-led does not mean talking throughout; a one-second reaction can establish authorship.
- End with a reason to see the next episode, not a generic "follow for more."
- Use Trial Reels for unfamiliar formats when available, while retaining normal organic controls [P03]. Instagram's stated recommendation framework and eligibility rules make original, eligible content the baseline [P01, P02].

### Carousel system

Publish one 6-9-slide carousel each week. Alternate between:

- **Design/process:** sketch -> rejected version -> chosen line -> two or three placement options -> close detail -> question.
- **Proof/result:** strongest finished or healed image -> movement reference -> stencil/placement -> outcome -> next-piece teaser.

Slide one must create a choice, identity, or transformation. Do not use a design sheet with no interpretation when the same asset can explain why the designs exist and where they move best.

### Caption and search system

Use three parts:

1. One-line tension or claim.
2. Two to four short sentences of artistic context.
3. One CTA.

Use descriptive language naturally: `abstract linework tattoo`, `dancing figure tattoo`, `contemporary tattoo art`, and `Copenhagen tattoo artist`. Instagram search considers text and profile signals, so clarity is preferable to a cloud of broad hashtags [P04].

### CTA allocation

Use only one primary CTA per feed item:

- 60% serial follow CTA: "Follow to see which placement becomes the final piece."
- 20% share CTA tied to identity: "Send this to the friend who moves like number two."
- 15% save CTA tied to utility: "Save this placement map for your next tattoo idea."
- 5% comment or vote CTA when the response changes a later episode.

Booking CTAs belong mainly in Stories, Highlights, and the bio. No more than one in five feed posts should lead with availability during the growth sprint.

### Community behavior

- Reply to meaningful comments within 24 hours.
- Turn one useful comment per week into a Reel or Story response.
- Leave five specific, non-promotional comments per week on target collaborators' work.
- Pin one comment that reinforces the series promise or asks the next useful question.

### Paid versus organic

The 332/181-follow Reels cannot set the organic benchmark because they were likely paid-assisted [A08]. Apply these rules:

1. Freeze a complete seven-day unboosted snapshot before any paid use: views, non-follower share, profile visits, attributed follows, and follows per 1,000 total views.
2. Spend nothing until the creative passes the organic validation gate in the calendar and 90-day plan. Paid reach must amplify evidence, not rescue weak creative.
3. In Month 1, deploy EUR 0 when nothing qualifies; if a creative qualifies, keep all paid testing to EUR 30-150 in total.
4. Once repeated organic winners exist, up to 60% of the budget actually deployed may amplify them. The EUR 250-750 monthly range is a ceiling, not a spending obligation, and unused money remains unspent.
5. Cap any one creative at EUR 150 before a fresh performance and fatigue review.
6. Record objective, audience, dates, spend, paid reach, paid profile visits, and paid follows separately. Never report a boosted total as organic growth.
7. Use paid results to learn distribution economics, not to declare content-market fit. Continue organic replications while paid tests run.

Production, collaboration materials, creator access, and measurable event/offline tests should be funded case by case only after core publishing is covered. Do not impose a rigid percentage allocation where no qualified opportunity exists.

Do not pay a creator before reviewing current audience geography, age, gender, non-follower reach, and prior Collab results [RESEARCH_SOURCES.md](RESEARCH_SOURCES.md).

## 9. TikTok Strategy

### Decision

TikTok should be a major **testing channel**, but not consume the majority of time. At about 20 followers there is no baseline and little downside to experimentation. TikTok's recommendation system is content-led, while Creator Search Insights can expose demand and content gaps [P08, P09].

### Exact cadence

- Publish three TikToks per week from the same raw capture used for Instagram until 30 posts have complete seven-day data.
- Adapt the first second, text, caption, and audio for TikTok rather than uploading an Instagram-watermarked export.
- Where possible, give one of the three a more native treatment: a comment response, search-led answer, raw studio thought, or alternate hook. It should still use shared footage rather than require a separate shoot.
- Apply the 30-post scale/maintain/pause gate before increasing time or adding spend.
- Publish only the 1-2 strongest clean edits per week to YouTube Shorts, and only when each title/opening adaptation takes 10 minutes or less. Shorts discovery depends on viewer response and relevance, but Shorts must remain optional reuse rather than a fixed burden [P13-P15].

### Adaptation rules

| Instagram version | TikTok adaptation |
|---|---|
| More polished visual sequence | Faster, direct opening with Amelie or the result |
| Profile-world context | Self-contained setup in one sentence |
| Saveable carousel companion | Searchable spoken question and visual answer |
| Brand-consistent caption | Plain search phrase plus one sentence |
| Audience vote in Stories | Ask in video, then answer a comment with the next video |

- Speak the search phrase: for example, "How I place abstract linework so it moves with the body."
- Use relevant terms and a small number of accurate hashtags; more hashtags do not guarantee broader distribution [P10].
- Use trends only when the sound or structure strengthens art, process, movement, or tattoo culture.
- Keep videos original and eligible for the For You feed [P11, P12].
- Do not watermark cross-posts.
- Do not make TikTok a second production operation until its organic follows per 1,000 views justify more time.

## 10. Viral Content Strategy

### Portfolio, not lottery

Apply the following mix across a rolling short-form portfolio. The percentages guide selection over time; they do not create a 20-piece production quota.

| Category | Share | Purpose | Example |
|---|---:|---|---|
| High probability | 40% | Repeat demonstrated interests | Movement-to-mark, design tension, placement reveal |
| High upside | 20% | Test culturally legible surprise | Tiny scale, unusual prompt, familiar idea reframed |
| Personality | 15% | Make Amelie memorable | Honest creative decision or opinion |
| Authority | 15% | Explain how the artist sees | Why a line or placement works |
| Community | 10% | Create participation and return | Vote, prompt, or result from a prior comment |

Cross-posts and platform treatments are not new concepts. The Instagram phase cadence and three-post TikTok pilot control volume; this mix controls what gets selected.

### Repeatable mechanisms

1. **Unresolved desire:** a one-off design exists, but no body or placement has been chosen.
2. **Transformation:** movement, memory, or prompt becomes line, placement, and final body art.
3. **Identity recognition:** a figure or phrase makes viewers identify themselves or a friend.
4. **Scale surprise:** unexpectedly tiny, large, or body-spanning interpretation.
5. **Constraint:** one line, one movement, one minute, one viewer prompt.
6. **Choice:** two drafts or three placements produce a consequential audience vote.
7. **Cultural bridge:** a familiar object, behavior, or creative discipline becomes an Amelie drawing.
8. **Payoff loop:** an earlier design returns healed or embodied, rewarding followers who saw episode one.

### Production loop

1. Capture one tattoo or drawing session as a reusable asset bank: face reaction, clean drawing, hand detail, stencil, body movement, reveal, and one spoken reflection.
2. Write three opening lines before editing; change one variable at a time.
3. Publish the strongest version organically on Instagram and a native variant on TikTok.
4. Review at 24 hours for obvious delivery issues and at seven days for a decision.
5. Repeat each promising format at least three times before scaling or killing it.
6. When a post wins, publish the next episode within seven days, update a pinned post if appropriate, and make the follow promise visible on the profile.

Virality without follower conversion is rented attention. The winning unit is organic non-follower reach multiplied by follow conversion and repeated across a recognizable series.

## 18. KPI Framework

The full definitions, tracking template, experiment thresholds, and channel model are in [`GROWTH_EXPERIMENTS_KPIS_AND_MODEL.md`](GROWTH_EXPERIMENTS_KPIS_AND_MODEL.md).

### North-star metrics

**Account level:** net follows per 1,000 total Instagram views.

`account net follows / total Instagram views x 1,000`

**Content level:** gross attributed follows per 1,000 total views, because post-level unfollows are unavailable.

`post-attributed gross follows / post views x 1,000`

Use non-follower share as a discovery diagnostic. If post-level non-follower views are available, follows per 1,000 non-follower views may be recorded as a secondary qualifier, but it does not replace the account-level planning denominator.

### Funnel metrics

| Stage | Metric | Formula or use |
|---|---|---|
| Attention | Views and accounts reached | Scale, reported separately |
| Discovery | Non-follower reach/view share | Whether distribution leaves the existing audience |
| Retention | Average watch time and completion rate | Compare only when duration is known |
| Transmission | Shares/reposts per 1,000 views | Whether people distribute the idea |
| Value | Saves per 1,000 views | Utility or reference value |
| Curiosity | Profile visits per 1,000 views | Hook-to-profile movement |
| Conversion | Gross content follows or net account follows per 1,000 total views; follows/profile visits | Acquisition efficiency, with the gross/net distinction labeled |
| Loyalty | Repeat viewers and Story engagement | Return behavior; currently missing |
| Business | Link taps and inquiries | Secondary, never the acquisition north star |

### Keep, scale, or kill

- Compare each post at the same seven-day age and keep paid and organic rows separate.
- Run a format three times before judgment unless it is ineligible, unsafe, or clearly off-positioning.
- **Scale:** the three-post median beats the rolling organic median on follows per 1,000 views and at least one leading metric such as non-follower share, profile visits, or shares.
- **Iterate:** a leading metric is strong but follows are weak. Fix profile promise, payoff, or CTA rather than discarding the reach mechanism.
- **Kill or repurpose:** three executions remain below the organic median for follow conversion and show no strong leading metric.
- Review weekly for production decisions and monthly for channel allocation. Do not make strategy decisions from first-hour views.

## 20. Kill List

Stop or sharply reduce:

1. Treating the 332/181-follow paid-assisted Reels as organic proof.
2. Boosting posts before organic validation or without a frozen seven-day organic snapshot and promotion-result records.

## 21. Five Biggest Strategic Bets

### 1. Own the movement-to-living-art category

- **Why it matters:** a named, repeatable worldview is harder to copy and gives non-clients a reason to follow.
- **Evidence:** Amelie's existing positioning centers movement, softness, collaboration, and art living on the body [A01]; global benchmarks show the value of a describable artistic world [C01-C05, C14].
- **Expected impact:** highest long-term effect on recognition, repeat viewing, and conversion.
- **Effort:** medium; requires consistent series packaging, not more tattoo sessions.
- **Execution:** publish `From Movement to Mark` weekly for 12 weeks with consistent title, opening grammar, and result loop.
- **KPI:** gross attributed follows per 1,000 total views, supported by non-follower share and repeat performance across three-post cohorts.

### 2. Validate face-led design tension organically

- **Why it matters:** this is the largest current follower signal, but paid assistance makes causality unknown.
- **Evidence:** 332 and 181 attributed follows from the two face-led Reels; likely boosts [A07, A08].
- **Expected impact:** potentially high near-term acquisition if the mechanism survives without paid distribution.
- **Effort:** low-medium.
- **Execution:** make three unboosted variants with different original designs, fixed structure, and a follow-to-result CTA; no promotion for seven days.
- **KPI:** organic follow conversion versus the rolling Reel median.

### 3. Make Amelie, not only the tattoos, recognizable

- **Why it matters:** style earns admiration; a visible artist and point of view create memory and return behavior.
- **Evidence:** creator benchmarks connect distinctive work with visible philosophy/personality [C05, C06, C09], and the current face-led concepts are the strongest acquisition hypothesis [A07].
- **Expected impact:** high conversion and repeat-audience potential.
- **Effort:** medium.
- **Execution:** show Amelie in roughly two-thirds of Reels, including at least one of two in Weeks 1-4, and keep personal disclosure tied to art and process.
- **KPI:** profile visits and follows per 1,000 views for face-present versus face-absent cohorts.

### 4. Use carousels as acquisition assets, not portfolio leftovers

- **Why it matters:** current posts dominate aggregate views/interactions, and design/placement posts are visible follower drivers.
- **Evidence:** posts generated 435,880 views and 15,411 interactions in the 90-day screen; a placement/design carousel generated 47 attributed follows [A07]. Volume is unknown, so per-post superiority is not claimed.
- **Expected impact:** medium-high, especially for saves, profile confidence, and follow conversion.
- **Effort:** low-medium because sessions already produce the assets.
- **Execution:** one narrative carousel weekly, always with a choice, transformation, or placement lesson.
- **KPI:** follows, profile visits, shares, and saves per 1,000 views.

### 5. Turn Copenhagen culture into distributed content

- **Why it matters:** local partners can provide borrowed trust, artifacts, venues, and PR while the content remains globally legible.
- **Evidence:** the source register identifies credible maker, design, event, and editorial routes [K01-K12, E01-E09, M01-M09]; current collaborations show local reach but weak transfer, so structure must improve [A07].
- **Expected impact:** medium with occasional high-upside spikes.
- **Effort:** medium-high.
- **Execution:** one Collab every 2-4 weeks, 3-5 tailored outreach actions weekly, and a trackable participatory activation only after the core engine is stable.
- **KPI:** attributable follows, partner-audience non-follower reach, profile visits, QR scans, and repeat partner performance.

## 22. Final Recommendation

Run a 90-day **creator growth sprint**, not a general social-media refresh.

### Non-negotiable operating system

- Weeks 1-4: two Instagram Reels and one carousel per week.
- Weeks 5-12: up to three Reels and one carousel per week, with the third Reel added only after repeat winners emerge and capacity permits.
- Three TikToks per week from shared footage until the 30-post gate, with one native treatment where possible.
- Only the 1-2 strongest clean edits as YouTube Shorts when each adaptation takes 10 minutes or less; no fixed Shorts obligation.
- Stories on 4-5 days per week.
- Amelie's face or voice in roughly two-thirds of Reels, including at least one of two in the base phase.
- One Instagram Collab every 2-4 weeks, contingent on a ready concept and partner.
- 3-5 tailored collaboration or PR outreach actions per week.
- One 45-minute analytics review each week.
- No paid spend before organic validation; Month 1 paid tests capped at EUR 30-150 in total; repeated winners may receive up to 60% of deployed budget; no obligation to spend; EUR 150 maximum per creative before review.

### First 14 days

1. Rewrite the bio and prepare the three pinned-post roles.
2. Create the organic tracking sheet and label the two historic outliers `likely paid-assisted`.
3. Publish four Instagram Reels and two carousels across the two weeks, using shared capture for `Movement Becomes A Line` and organic `The Unclaimed Original` tests.
4. Publish six TikToks from that shared footage, giving one per week a more native treatment where possible.
5. Adapt only the strongest clean edits to Shorts when each takes 10 minutes or less.
6. Complete 3-5 specific collaborator outreach actions each week, beginning with the best-fit verified prospects; do not assume a Collab will be ready in this period.
7. Spend nothing on paid distribution until the organic validation gate passes, then remain inside the Month 1 EUR 30-150 total test cap.
8. Secure client/media consent before any story-led production.

### Decision rule

At day 30, identify the two formats with the best repeated organic follow conversion; add a third weekly Reel from Week 5 only if those winners and available capacity justify it. At day 60, concentrate up to 70% of feed effort on validated formats and add measured collaboration, event, PR, and paid distribution only around qualified opportunities. At day 90, continue only the channels that produce attributable follower growth per hour and per euro.

The strategic priority is clear:

> Make Amelie's way of seeing recognizable, package it as recurring short-form stories, and distribute those stories through Instagram, TikTok, collaborators, and Copenhagen culture.

The account does not need more isolated portfolio posts. It needs a creator world whose next episode people choose to follow.
